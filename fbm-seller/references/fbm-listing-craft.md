# FBM Listing Craft

How to write listings that sell on Facebook Marketplace — titles, photos,
descriptions, and pricing tuned for local buyers.

## Titles

- Lead with what it is: **brand + model + key variant**. `Sony WH-1000XM5
  Noise Cancelling Headphones` beats `Headphones for sale`.
- Add condition in 1–2 words when it helps: `... — Like New`, `... (New in
  Box)`. Skip it if the condition field already covers it.
- One differentiator max: storage size, color, bundle. Not a spec sheet.
- No ALL CAPS, no emoji spam, no "MUST SEE!!!" — FBM's ranking doesn't
  reward it and buyers distrust it.

## Photos

- **First photo sells the click:** the item clean, well-lit, plain
  background, filling the frame. Shoot landscape.
- 4–8 photos: front, back, both sides, close-up of any flaw (honesty in the
  photos pre-empts haggling), what's included laid out together, and one
  "in use" or scale shot if size is ambiguous.
- Flaws get their own close-up — buyers who discover flaws at meetup either
  walk or renegotiate. Disclosed flaws hold the price.
- Don't use stock photos. Ever. FBM buyers assume stock photo = scam.

## Descriptions

- First line: what it is + condition in plain words.
- Then: what's included, key specs the buyer actually needs (size, capacity,
  compatibility), condition notes with flaws disclosed, reason for selling
  (one line — "upgraded" / "moving" builds trust).
- End with logistics: pickup area, `first come first served / no holds
  without deposit`, whether the price is firm or OBO.
- Short beats long. FBM buyers skim; a wall of text doesn't get read.
- **Grounded only.** Every claim must come from the owner or be unambiguous
  from the photos. When in doubt, leave it out.

## Pricing

FBM has **no sold data** — your comps are active listings, which skew high
(stale overpriced listings linger; fairly priced items sell fast and vanish).
Treat the active median as a **ceiling**, not a target.

- **Standard:** list ~10–15% above your walk-away price. FBM culture is
  haggling; buyers feel they won something and you land where you wanted.
- **Firm:** price at the walk-away number and write `Firm` in the title or
  first line. Use when the item is fairly priced and you don't want the
  dance — expect fewer but more serious inquiries.
- **OBO:** price at or slightly above the active median, mark `OBO`. Use
  for unusual items where the market is unclear.
- **Quick sale:** price 10–20% under the active median. Moves in days.
- No fees on local cash sales, so the list price is the take-home (minus
  any meetup hassle you price in).
- **Anchor against eBay sold comps** when FBM comps are thin: FBM local
  price ≈ eBay sold − shipping − fees the buyer avoids. Don't expect eBay
  prices on FBM — local buyers won't pay shipping-equivalent premiums.

## Negotiation norms

- Expect first offers at 60–70% of ask. Don't be offended; counter once,
  hold your floor.
- "Is this available?" → most are tire-kickers. Serious buyers ask specific
  questions or propose a meetup time.
- Never hold an item without a deposit — "I'll come tomorrow" usually
  means never. First person with cash (or e-transfer) gets it.
- Meet in public for high-value items; bring someone for anything over a
  few hundred dollars.

## Relist cadence

- FBM ranks by recency. A listing older than ~3–4 weeks is effectively
  invisible even if it's still "active".
- No inquiries in 7–14 days → propose a 5–10% price cut first (cheaper than
  relisting and keeps the listing's age signals).
- Still nothing after the cut, or 3–4 weeks old → delete and re-create
  for a fresh `creation_date`. Don't just edit the title — the timestamp is
  what matters.

## Safety

- Cash or e-transfer on pickup; never accept overpayment + refund (classic
  scam), never ship before payment clears, never share verification codes.
- Keep the exact home address out of the listing — neighborhood-level
  location only; share the meetup spot privately.
- If a buyer conversation smells wrong (overpayment, "my mover will pick
  it up", gift cards), tell the owner and stop engaging.
